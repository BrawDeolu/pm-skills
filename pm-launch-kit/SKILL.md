# PM Launch Kit

Turn shipped work into content that helps users find it and shows the PM's thinking. The audience is usually two groups at once: potential users and people who might hire or work with the PM.

## Step 1. Gather facts

- What shipped, for whom, and the problem it solves.
- Your role and the team's role. Use "we" for team work.
- Real outcomes only: adoption, time saved, customer quotes. **Never invent numbers.** If there are no results yet, talk about the problem and the decisions, not results.
- The link (store page, product page, demo video).
- What must not be said publicly (unreleased features, internal metrics, customer names without permission).

## Step 2. Pick the pieces

### LinkedIn post (2 variants)
- **Variant A, problem-led**: the user pain in the first line, what we built, one key decision and why, link.
- **Variant B, story-led**: a short moment (a customer call, a failed first attempt), the insight, what changed, link.
- First line must stand alone in the preview. No "I'm excited to announce".
- Show product judgement: a trade-off made, something cut, something learned from users.
- Short paragraphs, no hashtag walls (3 at most), no emoji bullets unless the person likes them.

### Portfolio case study
Problem, Users, Constraints, What I did (decisions, not tasks), Result or what we learned, Visuals. 300 to 600 words.

### Community posts (Reddit, product communities, Slack groups)
- Read the community's rules first; many ban self-promotion.
- Lead with value: what problem, who it is for, that it is free if it is.
- 3 title options naming specific pain points.

### Cover image
- A designed cover, not a plain screenshot: bold colour, large display type, device mockup or cropped UI, consistent brand colours.
- Sizes: 1200x627 (LinkedIn link), 1080x1350 (LinkedIn or Instagram portrait), 1920x1080 (video thumbnail).
- Blur any personal data in UI shown.
- Check nothing overlaps and text is readable small.

## Step 3. Check

- Every claim is true and public-safe.
- Same product name and link across all pieces.
- Tone sounds like the person, not like marketing copy.
