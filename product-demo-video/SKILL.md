# Product Demo Video

Make demo videos people watch to the end: a clear goal, a tight shot list, a voiceover that sounds like a person talking, and a cover that matches earlier videos.

## Step 1. Brief (keep it short)

Confirm:
1. **Goal**: launch announcement, feature how-to, sales demo, onboarding or teaching.
2. **Audience**: existing users, prospects, investors, students.
3. **Length**: 30 to 60 seconds for social, 1 to 3 minutes for a feature walkthrough, longer only for teaching.
4. **Source footage**: screen recording, prototype, or animated scenes built from scratch.
5. **Voice**: AI voice or their own. If AI, offer 3 or 4 short samples and let them pick.
6. **Series style**: if there are earlier demo videos, match their cover, colours, music and intro.
7. **Captions**: only add subtitles if asked. Do not add them by default.

## Step 2. Script first, visuals second

Write the voiceover as **spoken narration**, not on-screen labels.
- Talk like a person explaining to a colleague. Contractions, short sentences, natural connectors ("and", "so", "now") between ideas.
- Visuals support what is said. They do not need to match it word for word.
- Open with the problem or outcome in one line, not with "Welcome to...".
- One idea per sentence. One screen action per sentence.
- End with one clear call to action (try it, where to find it, link).
- Use "we" for team-built products unless the person says otherwise.
- Check product names for pronunciation. If a brand name is often misread by voices, say the product or feature name instead.

Then write a **shot list**: Scene | What is on screen | Voiceover line | Duration.

## Step 3. Prepare the demo account

- Use a demo account with realistic but fake data.
- **Blur or replace all personal data**: names, emails, phone numbers, API keys, balances, customer lists. Check every frame before export.
- Close notifications, hide bookmarks bars and unrelated tabs.

## Step 4. Voiceover

If using an AI voice:
- Fix pronunciation before generating everything. Say acronyms the way people say them ("API" as "A P I" is fine, "JSON" as "jason", "404" as "four oh four"). Preview tricky words first.
- Use punctuation to control pacing. Add short pauses between sentences and a longer one between scenes.
- Generate one clip per line so a single line can be redone without redoing the rest.
- Listen to the whole thing once before syncing to visuals. If it sounds read rather than spoken, rewrite the script, not just the voice settings.

Open-source text-to-speech options that work offline: Kokoro (natural neural voices) and Piper (fast, but spells out abbreviations unless you substitute them).

## Step 5. Assemble

- Sync each scene to its voiceover clip; let the visual hold half a second after the line ends.
- Music: low, calm bed (lofi or ambient) about 15 to 20 dB under the voice. No music with lyrics.
- Transitions: simple cuts or short fades. Zoom into the part of the screen being discussed.
- Export 1080p (1920x1080 for YouTube and LinkedIn; 1080x1920 for Reels, TikTok, Shorts). H.264 MP4.
- If building animated scenes in code, render a contact sheet of frames per scene and check for overlapping text before the full render.

## Step 6. Cover and post

**Cover / thumbnail** (1920x1080 or the platform's size):
- A designed cover, not a raw screenshot: bold background colour, large display type with the feature name, a device mockup or cropped UI.
- Keep it consistent with earlier covers in the series.
- Check nothing overlaps and text is readable at small size.

**Post copy**: write 2 variants (one short and punchy, one story-led) for the platform the person names. Lead with the user problem, not the feature list. Add the link last.

## Step 7. Pre-publish check

- Every claim in the voiceover matches what actually shipped (check release notes). Remove anything that is still in progress.
- No personal data visible in any frame.
- Audio levels even; no clipped words.
- Cover, video and post use the same feature name.
